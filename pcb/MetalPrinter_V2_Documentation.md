# Metal 3D Printer Control Board V2 — Hardware Documentation

**Design:** Daniele Ingrassia (InMachines Ingrassia)
**Schematic:** `MetalPrinterV2.pdf`, 9 sheets, Altium Designer, revision "2nd Revise"
**PCB silkscreen date:** 2026-06-15

> Reverse-written from the V2 schematic set and the board render. Verify values and part numbers against the Altium source/BOM before ordering or modifying hardware. Open points are collected at the end.

---

## 1. Overview

The Metal 3D Printer Control Board V2 is the single-board controller for an open-source **laser powder-bed metal 3D printer**. It combines three subsystems that are usually separate industrial boxes:

- A **grblHAL motion controller** driving five axes of *external* stepper drivers plus six isolated end-stop/door inputs.
- A **fiber-laser interface** implementing the full Raycus **RFL-C200A** DB25 control set (analog power, modulation, enables, interlocks, status inputs and RS-232), plus a secondary 24 V diode-laser channel (Laser Tree K60 class).
- A **galvo scanner interface** generating the **XY2-100** protocol for an RC1001-class scan head, including its switched ±15 V power.

The work is split across **three RP2350B microcontrollers** (CPU-A: motion, CPU-B: supervisor/comms, CPU-C: galvo + fiber laser), and the supervisor adds Ethernet, environment sensors (temperature, oxygen, particulate) and the usual OLSK-style machine peripherals.

## 3. Power Architecture

Single **24 V DC / 10 A** input on a screw terminal, double-fused, with a 0.02 Ω low-side shunt read by an INA22x current monitor (the monitor and its I²C pull-ups run from a switched 3.3 V/500 mA rail so the supervisor can power the sense domain independently). Bulk storage 4× 470 µF/35 V.

Derived rails:

- **5 V:** LM2596S-5.0 buck (33 µH shielded SMD inductor, 2200 µF + 1000 µF filtering). The sheet also carries the footprint/notes for a drop-in AC-DC 5 V/2 A PCB-mount module and the XL4015 application circuit as design references. A **5V-USB / 5V-PWR jumper** selects whether logic 5 V comes from USB or the power stage.
- **3.3 V:** one AZ1117-class LDO per CPU (3V3A/3V3B/3V3C domains).
- **±15 V (galvo):** "For +15 V" buck plus a symmetric −15 V stage (XL4015/MBRD1045 class), with jumpers for symmetric-supply configuration and **EX±15V** inputs so an external ±15 V PSU can be used instead. The ±15 V reaches the galvo connector only through relays **K6/K7** (SRD-24VDC-SLC + TPM40V FET drive, `PIO_PWR_RLY`), so the scan head is unpowered until firmware closes them.
- **~18 V (sensors):** LM317G-AA5R adjustable regulator for the end-stop sensor supply.
- **Distribution headers:** 24 V for lights, 24 V for FAN#1 and FAN#2, 5 V for the power LED.

## 4. Control Architecture

Three discrete RP2350B implementations (sheets 7–9), each with the same pattern: 12 MHz crystal, 4 MB QSPI flash (W25Q class), 5 V→3.3 V LDO, BOOT + RST buttons, vertical USB-C with ESD diodes → common-mode choke → series resistors, TVS array. all three carry the 8 MB PSRAM (U20 on CPU-A, U23 on CPU-B, U26 on CPU-C). An earlier revision of this
document said the PSRAM was DNP on CPU-A/B; that is wrong. The schematic fits it on all three sheets with no
DNP marking, and it is populated on all three on the assembled board. CPU-C is the one that *needs* it, for
scan-path buffering. The `DNP1`/`DNP2`/`DNP3` silkscreen near each BOOT button refers to the adjacent
resistor (R207/R219/R231), not to the PSRAM.

### 4.1 CPU-A — Motion (grblHAL)

Sheet note: *GRBL-HAL, 5 Axes, END Stops, Laser#1 (PWM & RLY)*.

- `STP/DIR/EN` ×5 (X, Y, Z, A, B) → 74HCT541 buffers → external-driver headers
- `LIMX/Y/Z/A/B`, `Door` — isolated inputs
- `Laser1_Rely` (relay enable) and `Laser1 PWM` (5 V logic PWM out)
- UART to CPU-B; ADC-capable GP40–46 spare

### 4.2 CPU-B — "The Main Controller"

Sheet note: *Main Control, PC USB, Ethernet, Sensors, RS422, RS485, RGBLED*.

- Two UARTs bridge CPU-A and CPU-C (`UART0/1 TX/RX _A/_C`)
- **Ethernet:** SPI0 → W5500 (`Eth_MOSI/MISO/SCLK/CSN/INTN/RSTn`), HR911105A MagJack with link/speed LEDs
- **RS-422:** GM490 (MAX490-compatible) full-duplex transceiver, DIP-switch termination
- **RS-485:** MAX485 with `Laser_on` chain (reference design for the laser display/sensor unit on sheet 2)
- **Sensors:** NTC3950 analog input, I²C oxygen-sensor header (SDA/SCL/GND/5V), SDS011 PM2.5/PM10 dust sensor over UART (`PM10_TX/RX`), INA22x current monitor
- **Storage/UI:** microSD on SPI1 + `CARD_DETECT`; RGB LED drivers (SI2303 P-FETs from 24 V for the external common-cathode header, plus an on-board 1.6×1.5 mm common-anode RGB LED); `Extern I/O` opto/LED channel; GPIO41–46 breakout header (P8)

### 4.3 CPU-C — Galvo + Fiber Laser

Sheet note: *Galvo-PIO (DB25 Port), Laser#2, 8MB PSRAM*.

- **XY2-100 generation in PIO:** `PIOCLOCK`, `PIOSYNC`, `PIOXDATA`, `PIOYDATA` → AM26LS31CDR → differential CLK±/SYNC±/XCH±/YCH± on the DB15
- **Galvo power:** `PIO_PWR_RLY` / `PIO_15V_RLY` close K6/K7 to feed ±15 V to the head
- **Fiber laser control set** (see §6): `Laser_PWM`, `Laser_MOD+`, `Laser_En`, `Interlock_RLY`, `LaserRemoteON`, `LaserPowerON`, `LaserEnRST`, `LaserRedLight`, status inputs `PowerON_IN`, `LaserON_IN`, `SystemON_IN`, `LaserErr_IN`, analog `0-3.3ADC_IN`, and `LaserUartRX/TX`
- J39A/J39B headers expose the PIO signals and GP40–46 ADC channels for debugging/expansion

## 5. Motion System

Unlike the OLSK laser board, there are **no on-board driver ICs**: each axis presents a 4-pin header (STEP, DIR, ENABLE, GND at buffered 5 V logic) intended for external stepper drivers — the schematic embeds a wiring illustration for a typical TB6600-class driver with its own motor power supply. Two 74HCT541 octal buffers translate and strengthen the ten step/dir lines plus enables; every signal has an indicator LED (STP/DIR/EN per axis, X through B), which makes bring-up and fault-finding visual. Axis B's sheet region also carries the user LED.

End stops follow the proven OLSK pattern, expanded to six channels (5 limits + door): ~18 V sensor supply, MST22D8G2 DIP switch per channel for NC/NO, RC filtering, EL3H7 optocoupler, status LED, 3.3 V output. Board note: *NC (PNP) sensors send +24 V when triggered; NO (NPN) sensors send 0 V (GND).*

## 6. Fiber Laser Interface (Raycus RFL-C200A, DB25)

Sheet 6 implements the complete Raycus control interface (the vendor's DB25 pin table is embedded in the schematic):

- **Power setting:** `PWM_IN` from CPU-C is converted to the laser's **0–10 V analog power input** by an LM358-based PWM-to-voltage converter (buffered, filtered, trimmed).
- **Modulation:** `MOD+` TTL modulation line (with MOD− return) for on/off gating during marks.
- **Digital controls via ULN2803 buffer (24 V domain):** `Laser_EN`, `RED_Ctrl` (visible red guide beam), `Err_RST` (fault reset), plus PWM enable.
- **Relay controls:** `REMOTE_ON` and `REMOTE_PWRON` (start/main-power emulation of the laser's key/start switches) and a dedicated **interlock relay** (`Interlock_RLY`) driving the DB25 interlock pin pairs (Interlock_1A/1B/2A/2B) — TPM40V FET + SRD/HK4100-class relays with flyback diodes.
- **Status inputs (opto-isolated, AGND_P14 domain):** `Power Is ON`, `Laser Is ON`, `System Is ON`, `Laser_Err` through EL3H7 optocouplers.
- **Analog feedback:** the laser's 0–10 V power-out is divided to a `0-3.3ADC` input.
- **RS-232:** SP3222 transceiver gives CPU-C a serial service channel (`RFL_UARTTX/RX`) to the laser.
- Schematic notes document the required power-on sequence (interlock pins closed, timing of REMOTE pins) from the Raycus user guide.

**Laser #1 (secondary 24 V channel):** a simple, robust chain — `Laser1_Rely` closes an SLA-24VDC-SLA power relay feeding 24 V to a CH5.08 terminal, and `Laser1 PWM` provides a 0/5 V logic PWM signal on an XH connector. Suited to a Laser Tree K60-class diode module (e.g. for marking/alignment duties).

## 7. Galvo Scanner Interface (XY2-100, DB15)

Sheet 5 documents the scan-head link end to end:

- **Protocol:** XY2-100 (CLOCK±, SYNC±, CHANX±, CHANY±). CPU-C's PIO generates the four single-ended signals; an **AM26LS31CDR** quad differential line driver (schematic note compares it with the legacy SN75174N) converts them to RS-422-level pairs.
- **Connector:** DB15 (D-SUB-DR-15PCM4CB) wired per the RC1001-V1 scan-head signal-port definition (embedded in the sheet, including the 8-core signal cable and the 4-core ±15 V power cable colour codes).
- **Power:** +15 V and −15 V pass through relays K6 and K7 (SRD-24VDC-SLC, TPM40V FET drive, `PIO_PWR_RLY`) to a dedicated power connector, so firmware sequences head power. Status LEDs on both rails.
- Reference specs for the RC1001 head (~4000 mm/s marking, ±...) are embedded for the machine integrator.

## 10. Firmware Notes

- **CPU-A:** grblHAL (RP2350), 5-axis + laser-mode configuration; step/dir timing tuned for the external drivers in use.
- **CPU-B:** supervisor — Ethernet/USB front-ends, environment monitoring (O₂, PM, NTC, input current), safety policy, storage, lighting, and the UART bridges. It is the natural owner of the door/interlock policy.
- **CPU-C:** hard-real-time galvo streaming — PIO state machines clock out XY2-100 at the head's rate while the 8 MB PSRAM buffers scan trajectories; it also sequences the Raycus power-up (interlocks → remote power → remote on → enable, per the embedded timing notes) and services the laser's RS-232.

## 11. Open Points / To Verify

1. The CPU-B sheet's current-sense block is titled *"…monitoring the 48V DC"* but the board has only a 24 V input — the title looks like a carry-over from the OLSK laser board; the circuit itself senses the 24 V input shunt.
2. Exact current-monitor part (INA226 vs INA220 footprint text is ambiguous at print resolution) — check the BOM.
3. The AC-DC 5 V/2 A module vs LM2596 buck: confirm which option is populated in production.
4. Galvo head model and cable pinout should be confirmed against the actual head shipped (sheet references RC1001-V1).
5. Whether the B axis is used as a second gantry motor, powder-recoater, or platform drive is a machine-level decision — the board treats it as a generic fifth axis.
6. Title-block revision text ("2nd Revise") and the 2026-06-15 silkscreen suggest schematic and PCB are the same generation, but a BOM cross-check is still advised.
